## 1. Who's across the table

The **#2 to the CFO**, deep in FP&A. New finance leadership (CFO **Kimberly DeTrask**,
ex-State Street, in seat since Oct 2025) arrived with an explicit mandate: a
**"future-looking, institutionally-sophisticated finance function."** Your buyer is the
person who has to *operationalise* that mandate — turn board-level language into working
systems. **You are the execution layer for the modernisation they were hired to lead.**

They are: controls-obsessed, sponsor-graded (Genstar-backed, PIK-levered — thinks in
adjusted EBITDA, synergy, cash conversion, covenant headroom, exit-readiness), and
genuinely keen on AI but using **none** today.

**One thing to never forget:** Apex already has a Chief AI Officer (Helen Wang) and a
client-facing AI platform (Apex Nova). If you pitch generic "AI for finance," you look
redundant. Your wedge is the **CFO's own back office** — Nova points outward at clients;
you point the same ambition **inward** at group finance.

## 2. The three sentences that land (open with these)

1. *"You run a monthly board pack for a sponsor-backed group built through acquisition,
   across dozens of entities and systems — and the slowest, most manual part is the
   variance commentary and pack assembly at the back of the close."*
2. *"AI can produce a reviewable first draft of that pack from your own numbers — with a
   full audit trail and you in control of every figure — turning days of writing into
   minutes of editing."*
3. *"That gives your team the back half of the month back for the analysis the board
   actually wants more of — margin by service line, synergy tracking, cash conversion."*

Then: *"We're not here to sell you a tool. We're here to show you the art of the possible,
and to be the partner who ships it. Let me show you."* → open the site.

## 4. Objection handling

| They say… | You say… |
|---|---|
| **"We already have a CAIO and Apex Nova."** | "Exactly — and that's a strength. Nova is client- and product-facing. We're the complement, not a competitor: we point that same thesis inward at the CFO's own back office — consolidation, board packs, FP&A. We'd work *with* Helen's team, not around it." |
| **"Our close and controls are sacred."** | "Agreed — and we don't touch them. Everything we do sits *after* the numbers are locked. Not one journal, control, or ledger changes. We automate the assembly and narrative, never the number." |
| **"AI hallucinates — I can't put that in front of a board."** | "That's why every output is a *draft for review*, fully sourced to the cell it came from, with a named human sign-off before anything leaves the room. The system fails safe — it says 'I don't know' rather than inventing a figure. Governance is in the build." |
| **"We've seen AI pilots go nowhere."** | "So has everyone — ~88% never reach production, because of scope and ownership, not technology. That's the exact gap we're built for. We're measured on one thing: agents in production with a controller's sign-off, in 90 days — not a pilot graveyard." |
| **"Why not just use Anaplan/Pigment's AI?"** | "A vendor's AI only sees data inside that vendor's walls. It won't reconcile across your ERP, warehouse, CRM and a decade of spreadsheets, and it deepens lock-in. We're paid by you, not a platform — we'll even tell you when *not* to buy the tool the vendor's selling." |
| **"Why not the Big 4?"** | "They sell you the strategy and hand you a deck at the handoff — slow, expensive, junior delivery. The person who scopes it with us is the person who ships it. We start on outcomes in 90 days, not after a six-figure strategy phase." |
| **"What's the ROI, really?"** | Open the calculator, put in *their* numbers. "This is deliberately conservative — netted for review time and a phased ramp. And it's capacity re-deployed to analysis, not headcount cut — the outcome your sponsors actually reward." |
| **"Is our data even ready?"** | "Poor data readiness is the #1 blocker in every survey — which is why we scope the lighthouse around data we *can* reach, and treat foundations as part of the build, not a prerequisite you fix first." |

## 5. Language — speak their tongue

**Use:** board pack, management pack, sponsor reporting; variance bridge, actual vs budget
vs forecast vs prior year; adjusted/normalised EBITDA, organic vs inorganic, synergy
realisation, cash conversion, covenant headroom, exit/IPO-grade; close cycle, WD5,
reforecast; single source of truth, consolidation, intercompany, FX translation;
human-in-the-loop, audit trail, controls, governance. Echo their leadership's own words:
**"institutional sophistication," "future-looking finance function."**

**Avoid:** "revolutionary," "autonomous," "replace your team," generic "AI for finance,"
anything implying AI produces final numbers unreviewed.

---

## 6. The ask (close on this)

**A six-week lighthouse on one high-value use case from the map — on your data, in your
house style, controls untouched.** Low risk, high signal. Pre-agree the baseline *they*
set, so the ROI is theirs, not ours. If it lands, we co-author the strategy and roadmap.

**Immediate next step:** a 90-minute working session to pick the one or two lighthouses and
agree the success metric. That's the whole yes you're asking for today.

---

## 7. Facts to be careful with (accuracy > impressiveness)

- **Don't state a hard acquisition count** — public sources range widely (20s to 40s). Say
  "grown through acquisition" / "a roll-up."
- Benchmarks are **third-party, directional** — attribute to the source, don't overclaim.
- The **~88%** last-mile figure is widely cited but from secondary sources — frame as
  "industry analyses suggest as high as ~88%," not gospel.
- Dashboard/ROI numbers are **illustrative/synthetic** — say so once, upfront. It builds
  trust, and the interactivity is the point, not the specific figures.
- You don't need to name the CFO out loud unless it lands naturally — the *mandate* is the
  hook, not the name-drop.
