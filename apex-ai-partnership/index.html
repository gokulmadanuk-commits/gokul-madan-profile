02 — Controls first

What we will never touch.

Your close is slow because it is careful. -plus acquired ledgers, multi-GAAP consolidation across countries, heavy intercompany, depositary and ManCo regulatory reporting, sponsor-grade covenant packs — the controls that make trustworthy were earned. So this proposal contains no close acceleration, no booking automation, and nothing that writes to the ledger. The close stays exactly as it is.

AI belongs before the close — preparation, drafts, reconciliation-ready inputs — and after it: commentary, packs, analysis, answers. That boundary is not a limitation of the technology. It is the design principle, and everything else in this document is built on it.

  • AI drafts. Humans approve.Nothing circulates without a named reviewer; the sign-off gate is part of the architecture, not the workflow guidance.
  • Grounded in your GL.Figures are retrieved from governed data, never generated — and every one cites the consolidation version it came from.
  • Auditable by design.Prompt and output logs retained; each draft traceable figure-by-figure; per-decision evidence of who approved what.
  • Private by default.A private tenant that never trains on your data, role-based access mirroring existing entitlements, retention windows you set.
  • Gated by evaluation.Pilots carry written success measures and kill criteria from day one. A pilot that fails, fails cheaply and on purpose.

Finance leaders will trust AI when they can audit it.

07 — Objections, answered

Answered before the Q&A.

Won’t it hallucinate?

It is the right first question — of CFOs report having seen hallucinated or incorrect AI output, and say human oversight is critical14. Our architecture assumes both numbers are permanent. Figures are never generated: they are retrieved from governed data and cited to source; narrative is drafted around them; and nothing circulates without a named human approving it. If a claim cannot cite its figure, it does not ship.

Where does our data go?

Nowhere. A private tenant in your jurisdiction of choice; no training on your data, contractually and architecturally; role-based access mirroring your existing entitlements; retention windows you set. Your security team gets the architecture diagram before any data does.

Will our auditors accept it?

Auditors accept what they can trace. Every draft carries figure-level lineage to the consolidation version it was built from; every prompt and output is logged and retained; the review gate produces per-decision evidence of who approved what. SOC 2 controls, decision logs, reproducible outputs — designed for the audit file, not retrofitted to it.

Does any of this touch the close?

No. This is section 02, and it is the one non-negotiable in this proposal: no journal entries, no booking adjustments, no “close acceleration”. AI works before the close on preparation, and after it on commentary, packs and analysis. stays — what changes is what happens in the days after it.

What if a pilot fails?

Then it fails in week six for the cost of week six — against kill criteria we wrote down before starting, with a measured reason why. That knowledge redirects the roadmap while the second candidate use case is already scoped. The expensive failure mode in AI is not the pilot that dies; it is the one that lingers for eighteen months because nobody defined what success meant.
